======================================================================
LEAVE-ONE-MEDIUM-OUT CROSS-VALIDATION REPORT
======================================================================

RESEARCH QUESTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Can ML models for corrosion inhibitors generalize across corrosive
environments, or are they environment-specific?

EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Train on 2 mediums, test on the 3rd (completely unseen medium)

RESULTS SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Experiment                     | RF R²    | SVR R²  
----------------------------------------------------------------------
HCl+NaCl → CPS                 | -2.102   | -0.434  
HCl+CPS → NaCl                 | -3.168   | -3.097  
NaCl+CPS → HCl                 | -6.343   | -6.766  
----------------------------------------------------------------------
AVERAGE (Leave-One-Out)        | -3.871   | -3.432  
General Model (15% test)       | 0.417    | 0.370   

KEY FINDINGS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. MODEL FAILS TO GENERALIZE ACROSS MEDIUMS
   Average R² < 0 indicates the model performs worse than
   predicting the mean. The model cannot extrapolate to unseen
   corrosive environments.

2. pH AS A PROXY FOR MEDIUM
   The three mediums have distinct, non-overlapping pH values:
   - HCl:  pH = 0.5  (acidic)
   - NaCl: pH = 4.9  (neutral)
   - CPS:  pH = 12.5 (alkaline)

   When testing on an unseen medium, the model must extrapolate
   to a pH range it never encountered during training.

3. IMPLICATIONS FOR MODEL DEPLOYMENT
   - The model should NOT be used to predict IE for mediums
     not present in the training data.
   - Medium-specific models or additional features may be needed
     to capture environment-dependent behavior.


CONCLUSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The leave-one-medium-out experiment reveals that the current model
does NOT generalize well to unseen corrosive environments. This
suggests that pH (which encodes medium type) is being used as a
proxy rather than the model learning transferable relationships
between molecular properties and inhibition efficiency.
